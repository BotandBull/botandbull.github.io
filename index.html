Building an automated trading bot, in public

I built a stock trading bot without knowing how to code, and I wrote down everything along the way. If you're thinking about trying it yourself, this is the order I'd read things in.

Where it started

A couple of years ago, spreadsheets and email were about as technical as I got. The first article is the honest version of how that changed: why I tried, what almost talked me out of it, and what it really took to go from nothing to a working trading bot with no coding background.

Getting it running

Once I decided to go for it, the hardest part was knowing what to do first. I wrote the setup out in the order I wish I'd followed: the account, the server, the first connection, and a long stretch of testing before any real money. That's all in how to set up a trading bot from scratch.

Later I wanted the bot to watch more than stocks, so I wrote up what changed when I added crypto to the same bot. If you're curious what everything runs on, I keep a running list of the tools behind the build.

Living with it day to day

Running a bot every market day taught me things a test run never did. The first surprise was how small the list of stocks it looked at really was, which led to widening the bot's watchlist.

The second was a day it didn't buy anything at all. I assumed it was broken. It wasn't, and why my trading bot bought nothing explains why a quiet day can be the bot doing its job.

And some days it really was broken. I kept notes on what went wrong and how I fixed it.

What I'd tell you before you start

If I could hand my past self one article, it would be what I'd do differently if I started over today. The mistakes themselves are split into two parts: the early mistakes nobody warns you about, and the errors that only show up once a bot is live.
